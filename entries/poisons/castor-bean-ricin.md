## Overview

Castor bean plants are widely grown ornamentally, and castor oil (from properly processed seeds) is a common industrial product. The raw seeds themselves contain ricin and pose a poisoning risk, particularly to children, if chewed or swallowed.

## Mechanism

Ricin blocks the cell machinery responsible for building proteins, which is lethal to affected cells over time rather than instantly.

## Symptoms

Whole, unchewed seeds are less dangerous than chewed ones, since the intact seed coat limits absorption — but any suspected ingestion still warrants medical evaluation.

## Treatment

There is no antidote; hospital care is supportive and focused on managing symptoms and monitoring organ function.
